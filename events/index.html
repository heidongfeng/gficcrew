What the crew does together

We keep ten meetup formats on the board, from parties and networking mixers through to food crawls, language exchange, hikes, sports leagues and Chinese cultural experiences. We meet up in person in Guangzhou and Shenzhen, and expats anywhere in China can join the online groups and our national group.

The formats we run

Nothing sits on a fixed calendar yet. These are the formats we build meetups around, and each one works a little differently depending on whether you have just landed or have been here for years.

Easy to turn up to on your own

The social formats with the lowest barrier. You can arrive alone, and there is already something to do and someone to talk to.

Parties and socials

The format everyone already knows how to enjoy.

Why it works
No shared hobby and no structured activity to sit through — a party does the work for you. Rooftop parties, BBQs, karaoke nights, wine tastings, club nights, and themed or holiday nights all put people in the same room with something to do. That is why they are both the easiest way in and the thing people keep coming back to.
What changes in China
The party is familiar, the surroundings are not. Which venues are used to international crowds, what a night actually costs, how to get home afterwards, and how to find the crowd in the first place. Someone who already knows skips the part where you guess.
If you come on your own
Turning up alone is normal here, not awkward: almost everyone at a party is there to meet people. Coming in with the crew means you are never the one left by the door.

Networking mixers

A room where everyone is there to meet someone new.

Why it works
A mixer gives you a reason to talk to a stranger before you have one — work, a side project, or simply being new in town. It suits people who would rather have a stated purpose than a purely social night, and it is where a lot of the practical help in the community first gets passed around.
What changes in China
Industry events happen in every big Chinese city, but they are scattered, often run in Chinese, and hard to find from the outside. A crew mixer pulls the same expat and internationally-minded crowd into one room and handles the introductions.
If you come on your own
Everyone arrives alone, so there is nothing to explain. The whole point of the format is that you do not know anyone yet.

Food crawls and shared meals

Nobody has to be interesting when there is food on the table.

Why it works
Eating together is the one thing every culture already agrees on. You point, you react, and that reaction becomes the conversation. Hands-on food like hotpot or dumplings works better than a formal dinner because everyone is busy doing something.
What changes in China
The food is why plenty of people came to China, but ordering it is a separate skill. Pictureless menus, dishes you cannot pronounce, and no reliable way to tell whether you chose well. Having someone order for the table turns a stressful meal into a good one.
If you come on your own
Eating alone is normal at home and draws attention in a lot of Chinese restaurants. Going with people removes the stares and gets you more dishes to try.

Language exchange

A reason to show up that is easier to say out loud than wanting friends.

Why it works
Saying you are here to practise Chinese is a much easier sentence than saying you are here to meet people. That is why quieter people turn up to these and never turn up to anything else. It also brings two crowds into one room: expats who want Chinese and locals who want English.
What changes in China
Demand is enormous and quality is uneven. Plenty of people have been to one that quietly became a dating event, or an English corner where nobody talks to anyone new. Sessions with a host, a structure and a topic are rare enough that you notice straight away.
If you come on your own
Almost nobody feels awkward arriving alone here, because the whole point is that you get paired with someone within minutes.

Worth planning ahead for

Bigger and more organized, and each one takes a bit more setting up than a night out does.

Hikes and weekend getaways

A whole day together does more than an evening does.

Why it works
Time is the thing that turns acquaintances into friends. On a day trip there is room for the conversation to get past where you are from and what you do. Westerners generally arrive already liking the outdoors, so there is nothing to sell.
What changes in China
There is a lot of scenery within reach of every big Chinese city, and getting to it means tickets, transport, sometimes a night somewhere, and a language gap at each step. Doing that alone on a Saturday is a big ask, which is why most people never do.
If you come on your own
Nobody joins a hike to talk to people they already know. Turning up alone is the normal way to do it.

Sports leagues and fitness

You can be useful before your Mandarin is good enough for small talk.

Why it works
Sport gives you a job on the pitch and something to talk about afterwards, so language stops being the barrier. The repeat factor is what sets it apart though. You see the same faces regularly instead of once, and that is how friendships actually form.
What changes in China
Courts and pitches are everywhere in Chinese cities. What eats your time is rounding up enough people, holding a booking, and making sure somebody comes next week. Most informal attempts fade out after two sessions for exactly this reason.
If you come on your own
Turning up alone is expected. Teams are short of players far more often than they are full.

Chinese cultural experiences

Curiosity is easy. Acting on it alone is not.

Why it works
Real interest in Chinese culture is common and hard to follow up on your own. You have to find someone who teaches it, get there, and then work out what you are actually looking at. In a session like this all of that is handled, and there are other people reacting alongside you.
What changes in China
Being run by locals is the genuine edge here. A Chinese host explaining dumplings, calligraphy or tea is not putting on a show for visitors, they are explaining something they grew up with, and people pick up on that difference immediately.
If you come on your own
These are structured enough that you are never left standing in a corner wondering who to talk to.

Smaller and more specific

Narrower interests, or formats that work best once you already recognise a few faces.

Board games and trivia nights

The game supplies the conversation so you do not have to.

Why it works
Structure is the whole appeal. Nobody has to improvise small talk because there is something on the table that everyone is looking at. It suits people who are new in town, or who are not going to walk up to a stranger and introduce themselves.
What changes in China
It needs games in English and most shops stock Chinese versions, so somebody has to bring them. A bit more setup than everything above, which is the only reason it sits lower.
If you come on your own
Good first event. You spend the first twenty minutes learning rules rather than making conversation.

Coffee chats and brunch

A low-commitment first step rather than the main event.

Why it works
Brunch is a real cultural touchstone for plenty of Westerners and an easy first meeting. Nobody has to commit their whole evening and nobody has to drink.
What changes in China
Coffee here is a fast transaction and brunch crowds exist mainly in the biggest cities, so it carries less weight than it does at home. Useful as a gentle entry, weak as a centrepiece.
If you come on your own
Sitting in a cafe with people you have not met yet costs you an hour and nothing else.

Interest groups: books, photography, film

What keeps people coming back rather than what brings them in.

Why it works
Smaller groups, tighter bonds, and much better suited to people who already know a few faces. Photography walks in particular give you something to look at together, which takes the pressure off the conversation.
What changes in China
Retention more than recruitment. It matters once somebody has settled in and wants a reason to keep showing up.
If you come on your own
Joining alone is fine, though these work better once you already recognise somebody in the room.
